### Practitioner Review Summary

The practitioner review was accepted primarily as **limitations and future-work feedback** rather than as a reason to modify the frozen V1 experiment.

The review identified four main areas:

1. **Evaluation limitations** — small, specialized, artificially balanced dataset and final-action-only evaluation.
2. **Human factors** — SAFE may create false reassurance and HOLD currently lacks a real reviewer workflow.
3. **Interpretation limitations** — probability estimates and decision costs should not be treated as empirically validated real-world quantities.
4. **Deployment engineering gaps** — privacy controls, adversarial-input handling, checkpointing, reproducibility metadata, and operational monitoring remain unimplemented.

No V1 prompt, policy threshold, dataset label, or recorded prediction was changed after reviewing this feedback.

### Probability Review Summary

The probability review identified a central limitation of V1:

**`scamProbability` should currently be interpreted as an LLM-generated risk score rather than a validated Bayesian posterior probability.**

The review supports keeping three concepts separate:

* fraudulent intent
* risk of the requested action
* identity/organization verification

It also found that the current probability thresholds are hand-designed rather than derived from the cost matrix, while the current calibration dataset is too small to justify interpreting raw scores as precise real-world probabilities.

The suggested threshold analysis was **not applied to V1** because changing thresholds after observing the evaluation results could introduce overfitting.

Future work should consider:

* explicit priors supported by deployment data
* empirically estimated likelihoods
* held-out calibration data
* Brier score and log loss
* timestep-level calibration
* balanced evidence tracking
* explicit identity-verification state

No V1 probability, threshold, policy, label, or recorded prediction was modified after this review.

### Preprint Review Summary

The reviewer judged the project suitable as an **exploratory pilot after revision**, but not yet as evidence of general recruitment-scam detection effectiveness.

The strongest defensible contribution is:

> A small study of how sequential uncertainty estimates and action-risk policies change recruitment-scam decisions under partial observability.

The review recommends avoiding claims that:

* HOLD has been shown to reduce real-world harm.
* The system has demonstrated early scam detection.
* The raw scores are calibrated probabilities.
* The model itself performs Bayesian updating.
* Generated reasoning summaries establish the internal cause of an error.

Instead, the preprint should report these as:

* observed experimental outcomes,
* modeling assumptions,
* hypotheses suggested by the results,
* and open questions for future evaluation.
